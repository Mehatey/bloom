3 · CONTENT DECISIONS I MADE FOR YOU

You said you didn't know — here are the calls. Override anything that doesn't feel right.

3a — What to engrave on the laser

Pick 5 mandalas from the show's scene library that translate to line-art best:

  1. Flower of Life — universal, classic sacred geometry. Engrave on the cube.
  2. Rose mandala (12-fold) — your beauty chapter. Engrave on a wood disc to sit on the turntable.
  3. Golden phyllotaxis — the bloom chapter. Spiral inward. Wood plaque on entry wall.
  4. Celtic interwoven (4-pointed knot) — patterns chapter. Engrave on acrylic; backlit.
  5. Lunar phase ring — impermanence. Engrave on mirror or acrylic.

SVGs for all five are in print_kit/mandala_svgs/ and ready for the laser.

3b — What to engrave the TEXT pieces with

The text engravings hold both halves of your work — the meditation half AND the surveillance critique. Pair them.

  • Meditation side: "you have always been bloom."
  • Critique side: "be here now · be sold to · be tracked"
  • Cork wall: "what was beautiful · who has held you · what can you put down"
  • Entry placard: "a meditation that listens"
  • Closing: "you are not a notification."

These read like haiku when set in Instrument Serif. They are the work's language in object form.

3c — Should the printed media be quotes or imagery?

Both, divided by surface.

  • Mandalas on opaque surfaces (wood, mirror, acrylic) — beauty, anchored, lit.
  • Text on translucent surfaces (die-cut film, mylar, red filter) — language as overlay, layered, revealed.

This mirrors your thesis: the body is anchored; the message floats.

3d — A1 logo?

Yes — A1. Above the room's entrance. It is the first contact. Big enough to be a brand statement, small enough that the room is the experience, not the marketing. Also print one A4 backup for the wall card.

3e — Lenticular sheets?

SKIP for now. The novelty fights the meditation tone. Save them for one VERY specific moment if you find one (e.g., a portrait that flickers between "calm self" and "surveilled self") — but only if it lands. Otherwise they're a distraction.

3f — Business card design

Two-sided. Front: holographic BLOOM logo only. Back: name + email + URL + tagline. White-on-black or cream-on-black. Cardstock with a velvet finish if available.

4 · INSTALLATION — HOW EACH PHYSICAL OBJECT IS USED

Walking through Room 1205 from entry to back:

A · ENTRY (the threshold)

  • A1 holographic BLOOM logo — above the door
  • A2 placard — chest-height, beside the door. "A meditation that listens."
  • A small bowl of business cards — on a plinth or shelf for visitors to take.

B · CENTER (the chair)

  • Chair facing 75-inch TV
  • BrainBit station — small side table with attendant chair
  • Behind the chair: BLUE FIRE GLASS in a Fibonacci spiral on the wall. Use clear silicone adhesive. Spiral mimics scene 13 (golden phyllotaxis). Light from below with a single warm LED uplight.

C · BACK WALL (the cork wall)

  • 3 question cards at top — A4 each, evenly spaced
  • Cards + pens + pushpins in three small bowls beneath
  • Cork wall grows through the night — visitors pin their cards

D · THE ALTAR (coda corner)

  • Glass cube on the turntable — turntable rotates slowly (60 RPM too fast — use 33⅓ or even slower if possible). Cube refracts the projection.
  • Two two-way mirrors face each other on either side of the cube — INFINITY EFFECT looking inward. Visitors look from the entry side toward the back; the mirrors create a tunnel of cubes.
  • Vision Pro on a small stool beside it for the AR coda — IMG_3825 marker on the altar surface.

E · CEILING — layers of light

  • MYLAR — cut into 6-8 strips, 8-10 ft long, twisted gently. Hang from ceiling in irregular cluster above the chair. Catches projection scatter + ambient light. If you have time: cut tiny mandala/flower-of-life holes in the strips with hole punch or laser — light dances through.
  • FRESNEL LENS — drill a single hole at the top edge. Hang from ceiling on monofilament at eye level when seated. Place it so when the visitor leans forward, the lens magnifies the wall message behind it. This is the surveillance moment — what looks like meditation becomes magnification, scrutiny, the watching eye.

F · HIDDEN LAYERS (the critique, revealed)

  • RED PHOTO FILTER PAPER — print decoy text on the wall (looks like calm wellness language). Print the REAL message in cyan/red dot patterns that disappear unless viewed through the red filter. Provide one filter card on the entry table: "hold this up to the wall." Examples below in section 5.
  • DIE-CUT FILM — print "be here now" repeating in concentric rings on clear film. Hang in front of the chair near the screen at an angle. Visitors see the meditation through the surveillance language. Pure overlap.
