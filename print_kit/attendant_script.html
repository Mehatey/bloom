Attendant Script.

For whoever is helping a visitor sit · Friday + Saturday

Before doors open

Check: laptop running complete.html?show=1 · BrainBit charged + connected · Kinect powered on · idle screen showing on TV · 200 blank cards stacked · pens · pushpins · red filter cards on entry table.

When a visitor approaches the room

01
Smile. Let them read the placards first. Don't rush them. Some people want context before they sit. Some want to dive in. "Hi. Take your time reading. Let me know when you're ready."
02
Invite them into the chair. "Have a seat in the chair. Get comfortable. You'll be here for about six minutes."
03
Place the BrainBit headband. Two sensors on the forehead, just above the eyebrows. Adjust the back strap so it's snug but not tight. Ask about glasses. "This is a brain sensor. It just reads your stillness — it can't read your thoughts. Tell me if it feels too tight." If the visitor seems nervous: "Most people forget they're wearing it within thirty seconds."
04
Step back. Press begin. Either on the laptop or via remote. The overstim plays first — about 40 seconds of chaos — then the meditation begins. "It opens with something loud. Let it pass. The quiet part starts after."
05
Hold space for 6 minutes. Stand to the side. Don't watch them — give them privacy. Look at your phone, look at the wall, look down. Be available but invisible. If the visitor cries, breathes hard, or laughs — that's the work happening. Don't intervene.
06
When the closing breath ring appears, approach gently. Wait for the visitor to open their eyes. Don't speak until they do. "Take your time."
07
Help them remove the BrainBit. Slide it forward. Place it back on the charging tray. "Thank you for sitting."
08
Point to the cork wall + altar. "There are three questions on the wall. Take a card, write one thing, pin it anywhere. The altar in the corner has a second piece — about ninety seconds — if you want to stay longer."
09
Offer a thank-you card on their way out. Hand it to them or point to the stack. "This is yours to take. Thank you for being here."

If something happens.

Troubleshooting · tone · the work behind the work

Things that might break

Quick reference

BrainBit won't connectReload the page with ?brain=force — uses simulated signal. Visitor experience is unchanged.
Kinect freezesScenes default to ambient values. Lose lean / hand response but story arc intact. Don't restart mid-session.
Audio cuts outReload tab. Backup: ?noaudio=1 for silent run.
Scene won't advanceWait 30 seconds. If still stuck, refresh — visitor will need to sit through it again. Apologize. It happens.
Visitor wants to leave mid-pieceDon't stop them. Help remove the BrainBit, thank them, point to the cork wall on the way out.
Visitor starts cryingStay in the room. Don't approach. Have a tissue ready for after.
Cork wall fills upDon't remove anything. Let cards overlap. The accumulation IS the piece.
Cards run out200 should last the show. If not — blank 3×5 index cards from any office store.
Big line formsTell people they can come back at any time during the show window. Hand out thank-you cards while they wait.

Tone — how to be

The piece is about listening. You are part of that. Don't sell it. Don't explain it. Don't apologize for it. Let it speak.

Speak softly. Move slowly. Stand still. If you don't know what to say, say nothing.

The visitor is not a customer. They are a guest. The room is theirs for six minutes.

If a visitor asks "what is this?"

say
"It's a meditation that listens. The headband senses your stillness. The room responds. It's about what you are when no one is watching." If they want more: point to the WHY placard. Let them read.

If a visitor asks "what's the point?"

say
"There isn't one. Just sit. The point is whatever you bring." This is honest. Don't oversell.

At the end of the night

Don't pack up the cork wall. Photograph it first — wide shot + close-ups. The cards become the show's analog memory. Save them all.

Power down the laptop. Charge the BrainBit. Eat something. Be proud.
