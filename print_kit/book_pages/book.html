— 04 —
the work

A meditation
that listens.

A seated installation in three parts. A 6-minute body-driven visual journey. A satirical overstimulation opening. A cork wall for handwritten reflection.

The body controls the room. The room responds with patterns. The room is also a critique of every wellness app you have ever been sold.

— 05 —

Sit in the chair. A BrainBit EEG senses your stillness, your focus, your alpha rhythm. A Microsoft Kinect notices your body — your lean, your hand, your motion energy.

A 75-inch screen unfolds in fifteen scenes across nine chapters: nebulae, dividing cells, sacred geometry, sand mandalas, lunar phases, golden phyllotaxis, lichtenberg lightning, breath.

Each scene is tuned to a chapter of pre-recorded narration in a Watts / Sagan register — soft authority without instruction.

— 06 —
origin

Before the code,
the conversation.

It started as a question I asked twenty people: when have you felt the spirit?

The answers — at a concert · holding my grandmother's hand · looking up at stars in Idaho · when I make something · in the silence after a song ends · in a kitchen with people I love — became the seed of every scene in this work.

— 07 —

workshop · spring 2026 · mind-mapping moments I felt the spirit

— 08 —

A mind-map taped to a studio wall. A sheet of butcher paper with twenty fragments of nirvana · nothing · somewhere · somebody.

Drawings of cages and bodies and questions: who am I · what do you need · want · to become?

Software was never the point. The software is the room I built for the question to live in.

— 09 —
process

Fifty modes.
Fifteen chosen.

Over the studio year I built roughly fifty generative visual modes. Each draws to a single canvas, each accepts inputs from the BrainBit (stillness, focus, alpha) and the Kinect (hand, lean, motion energy).

Celtic interwoven · Flower of Life · rose · sand · lunar phases · golden phyllotaxis · fractal Lichtenberg branches · bioluminescent organic glow · volumetric ray-marched nebulae.

— 15 —
the other half

The overstim
before the bloom.

Before the visitor can rest, they are shown what they have been resting from.

Forty seconds of overstimulation — Times Square walkers, news, ads, surveillance, AI weirdness, a wellness commercial — ending with the popup "You" Are Not Enough · Install.

The audience watches Install get pressed. What they receive is Bloom — a wellness app that holds them.

— 16 —

walkers · times square

YOLOv8 + ByteTrack identifies every pedestrian. Thirty-four percent get a hidden story:

going through a breakup
missing their grandmother
in love for the first time
just got fired
writing a novel they will never finish

Every body in the frame is a whole life the surveillance camera does not see.

— 17 —

be here now

A single CCTV camera scans the room. The phrase be here now spawns at the visitor's hand position — in soft pastel typography.

Wellness commodified back into surveillance language. The sentences are the same sentences.

The visitor rotates the camera by leaning. The text accumulates, fades, accumulates.

— 19 —

voices from the wall

Three questions on three cards. Two hundred blank cards beneath them. Each visitor writes one. By Saturday night the wall is the show.

"my grandmother's kitchen on a Sunday."
"a stranger paying for my coffee."
"the way the sky looked at five am."
"the weight of my phone. for one day."

— 21 —

The final words of the meditation are a closing release — a slow exhalation, a permission. The visitor opens their eyes.

The closing trajectory of their own brain-stillness from the last six minutes appears as a single golden line traced across the screen. It fades.

The visitor stands, exits past the cork wall, and pins a card.

The work is finally a small argument for staying.
